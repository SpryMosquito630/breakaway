# Genuine drill photo provenance

All 30 built-in drills have distinct real photographic media: 28 still references here and two existing filmed-example posters under `assets/drill-demos`. There are no generated human images, schematic photo substitutes, repeated stills, or alternative crops of the same photograph assigned to different drills. Custom drills inherit none.

A still can show the relevant setup, contact surface, receiving position or handling phase. It does not establish the complete movement, a player’s foot dominance, a coach review, measured technique, or endorsement. Follow each drill’s written steps. Individual limits are recorded in `provenance.json` and the app’s photo description.

## Transformations and quality

All assets were visually inspected as actual photographs or genuine video excerpts. No generative changes or artificial upscaling were applied. `provenance.json` records source-rendition hashes, exact source crop coordinates, film timestamps or storyboard frame identities, dimensions, output SHA256, byte sizes and contextual limits. Full-resolution research originals/videos are not shipped.

Images are at most 900px wide and under 120 KiB each. `wall.webp` and `wall-open.webp` are native 240 × 180 crops retaining player, ball and rebound wall, so they look softer in a large detail view. `l-turn.webp` is a native 440 × 330 macro contact photograph; the enlarged ball is intentionally only partly framed. Other size limits remain explicit per asset. Step-over uses a 900 × 675 genuine setup photograph, not the superseded low-resolution academic figure.

Photo payload: **1,435,950 bytes** (1.37 MiB), excluding the two existing film posters/videos.

`tests/drill-media.test.ts` checks all 30 built-ins including keeper drills, distinct source/asset identities, duplicate output hashes, exact registry references, provenance hashes and budgets without loading React Native assets.
