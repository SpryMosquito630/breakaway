The source was already licensed for the existing wall-pass example; this review inspected a different interval for across-body reception and an opposite-foot return.

The primary YouTube metadata was fetched again on November 14 and explicitly records `Creative Commons Attribution license (reuse allowed)`. [YouTube's licensing explanation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468?hl=en) explains attribution and reuse; the app retains original title, author, source, license and changes. Consistent with the prior source record, [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/) is used conservatively for this pre-August-2025 upload; the metadata does not separately encode a license version. No endorsement or independent model-release document is asserted.

The full 60.2-second source was inspected in chronological one-second frames. The 47–58-second interval was inspected at four frames per second, then 51–54 seconds at six frames per second to check receiving/return foot order. The selected **52.7–56.5 seconds** contains an incoming wall rebound, a receiving touch across the body, an opposite-foot grounded return and a repeat. There is no edit inside the chosen interval. The filmed repetition goes in one direction and at a quicker pace with markers: visible app notes tell learners to alternate first-touch direction and pass gently according to the written stages. It is a core-action example, not verification of every authored stage.

The muted H.264 export is 854×480 at 24 fps, 3.833 seconds. `ffprobe` confirms the expected stream and no audio; full decoding of three consecutive loops succeeds. The repeat has a visible reset and is not presented as seamless. The full-frame poster was visually inspected. Exact source/output/poster SHA256, source bounds, license metadata, metadata-file hash and transformations are retained in `assets/drill-clips/provenance.json`. The app registry points to bundled media, without an external player dependency.
